Warning color guide
  • Red · high scatter (CSD >10°).
  • Orange · CSD >4° through 10°.
  • Magenta · low moment; “very low” means ≤0.1% of the reference moment. Log-unit drops show the size of the dip.
  • Purple · possible loading flip or specimen swap; inspect orientation and identity.
  • Blue · source quality history; a previous bad flag needs context.

Colors indicate review priority and the kind of evidence, not calibrated error probabilities. Red takes precedence in the case banner; each reason retains its own color. Your decision remains separate from warning severity.

How to read the Zijderveld plot ↗

Which loading flip?

RAPID Up ↔ Down correction (X): use for a specimen loaded in the opposite Up/Down orientation from the declaration. This is the standard RAPID correction for the turnover you showed: marked top becomes bottom, with the side-arrow reference kept facing the same way. It keeps X and reverses Y and Z: D → −D (wrapped to 0–360°), I → −I. Moment is unchanged.

Other turnover (Y): a different half-turn that keeps Y and reverses X and Z: D → 180° − D, I → −I. This is an alternative misorientation, not the standard RAPID Up/Down correction.

Verified source: original Caltech RAPID/Paleomag Visual Basic v2.4.0 (2007), MeasurementBlock.cls, isUp and CorrectedSample. Setting Down changes the sign of Y and Z while leaving X unchanged in all four measurement positions. This verifies the released software convention; a locally modified lab version should be checked separately. Preview before approving; correctly declared Down measurements are already corrected by RAPID and must not be flipped again.

    Reading the Zijderveld plot
    

    A measurement is a 3D magnetic vector: its direction tells you where it points; its moment tells you its strength. This plot shows two views of that same vector.
    

    • Filled circles show the horizontal projection; open squares show the vertical projection. Choose X–Z / X–Y · N–S convention or Y–Z / Y–(−X) · E–W convention. Positive Y/East and Z/Down plot downward, as in PmagPy. For the E–W view, positive X plots upward. Labels use specimen axes; no geographic orientation is assumed. Horizontal projections use red filled circles; vertical projections use blue open squares. Temperature labels identify representative steps. Coordinates are in the specimen frame.
      • 

    • Distance from the origin: weaker moments usually lie closer to (0, 0). A projection can also approach the origin because the vector points mostly along the axis hidden from that view. Check both views and the moment plot.
      • 

    • Scale: vectors are divided by M₀, the first retained observation’s moment (shown below the plot). X and Y/−Z use equal scales. Zooming keeps the same M₀. The frame fits the displayed vectors and includes the origin.
      • 

    • Stereonet temperature colors: yellow → orange → red runs from the specimen’s coolest to hottest treatment. Lines connect observations in treatment order (repeats by time), with gaps at excluded measurements. These are visual connectors, not fitted paths or measures of spherical distance. The color scale stays fixed when zooming.
    • Connected points: observations ordered by treatment temperature, with repeats ordered by time. Choose a full trajectory or nearby observations. They are context, not a fitted demagnetization component. A straight segment may indicate a consistent component; a turn can reflect multiple components or an error.
      • 

    • Pink halo and SELECTED label: the measurement currently under review, marked on every plot, even when excluded. H and V identify its horizontal and vertical projections. Teal diamond: proposed rotation or the swap partner’s vector. A flip changes direction but preserves moment. A swap may change both. In the screened view, confirmed corrections are teal, original/excluded points are faded red, and lines break at excluded observations. Rejecting a proposal retains the measurement; it does not mark that measurement bad.
