    Reading the Zijderveld plot
    

    A measurement is a 3D magnetic vector: its direction tells you where it points; its moment tells you its strength. This plot shows two views of that same vector.
    

    • X–Y is the horizontal projection. X–(−Z) is the vertical projection; negative Z is plotted upward. Coordinates are in the specimen frame.
      • 

    • Distance from the origin: weaker moments usually lie closer to (0, 0). A projection can also approach the origin because the vector points mostly along the axis hidden from that view. Check both views and the moment plot.
      • 

    • Scale: vectors are divided by the largest moment in this local window. X and Y/−Z use equal scales. Different specimens and windows have different normalizations.
      • 

    • Connected points: nearby observations in time, including repeats. They are context, not a fitted demagnetization component. A straight segment may indicate a consistent component; a turn can reflect multiple components or an error.
      • 

    • Red square: recorded observation. Teal diamond: proposed rotation or the swap partner’s vector. A flip changes direction but preserves moment. A swap may change both.
    

    What each flag means
      

    • Low moment: much weaker than reliable observations of this specimen at comparable temperatures. A dropped or poorly positioned sample is one possibility. Genuine demagnetization also reduces moment; weakness alone does not prove an error.
      • 

    • CSD: angular scatter within the measurement, in degrees. Orange is >4° through 10°; red is >10°. High scatter suggests checking repeatability and signal strength. It does not by itself identify a flip or swap.
      • 

    • Possible loading flip: an X- or Y-axis half-turn improves agreement with local reference directions. Confirm the instrument’s loading axes and lab notes before accepting.
      • 

    • Possible swap: exchanging two nearby specimen observations improves agreement for both specimens. Inspect both sets of plots and the measurement sequence.
      • 

    • Existing bad flag: the source already marks this observation bad. Review its provenance before making a persistent exclusion.
    

    Screening thresholds come from the bundle’s rules. These are review hypotheses, not proof. Approve only when the evidence supports the proposed action; reject to retain it, or leave undecided.
